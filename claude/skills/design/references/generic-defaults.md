# Generic defaults

## Why defaults appear

When the brief leaves an axis free and an item from the lists below appears without a reason tied to this subject, treat it as a sign that no decision was made, and rewrite the element instead of softening it, because without direction the most common patterns win and outputs converge on the same look regardless of subject. Every item is legitimate for some brief, and when the brief asks for one, use it.

<example>
A centered hero with two buttons and a gradient blob on a tax-filing tool is rewritten around the filing deadline and the document checklist, not given a softer gradient.
</example>

Choose the replacement from the subject and not from the next most common alternative, and do not rotate through a private list of favorite fonts or palettes, because steering away from one common choice with a fixed list of alternatives moves the convergence onto the list. Fit to the subject is the goal, not variety for its own sake.

<example>
After dropping `Inter` for a ferry timetable, pick a face from the signage and print world of ferries, and not the same trendy grotesque every other generated page lands on.
</example>

Fix the functional defaults before the visual ones, because they break use and not only taste.

<example>
A dashboard with a refined palette but no empty or error state gets its `No results` and `Could not load` states built before any further polish.
</example>

## Composition defaults

- A centered hero with a headline, a gray subheading, two buttons, and a decorative gradient blob, followed by a row of three identical icon cards.
- The hero metric template of a big number, a small label, supporting stats, and a gradient accent.
- Content chopped into same-size rounded cards with one radius, one soft gray shadow, and equal padding everywhere. Nested cards are almost never right.
- A tracked uppercase eyebrow label above every heading, or numbered markers such as `01` and `02` on content that is not a sequence.
- Every section entering with the same fade and slide on scroll.
- A print-inspired look of hairline rules, zero radius, and dense multi-column text applied to a subject with no reason to look printed.
- A modal for a task that needs neither interruption nor protected focus.
- A fake logo wall, invented testimonials, or placeholder statistics.

## Surface defaults

- Purple or indigo to blue gradients on white, gradient text, and gradient washes used as decoration.
- A warm cream background with a high-contrast serif and a terracotta accent, a combination many generated pages share.
- A near-black background with one acid green or vermilion accent.
- Glassmorphism and backdrop blur as ornament rather than as a layer that separates controls from content.
- Zero-offset colored glows, hard offset block shadows outside a deliberately brutalist world, and the same `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadow under every element.
- A thick colored left border on cards, callouts, and list items.
- Emoji or Unicode glyphs standing in for icons, or icons mixed from several sets with different stroke weights.
- Near-black such as `#0B0B0B` standing in for a designed dark color, and pure gray text on colored surfaces.

## Typography and copy defaults

- Inter, Roboto, or the system sans chosen by reflex on a persuasive surface. A familiar sans is often right for Operate surfaces and native apps, so the tell is the absence of a decision, not the family itself.
- Accenting one word of a headline with italic, bold, or a different color.
- Monospace used as a costume for "technical" on labels that are not code, data, or measurements.
- Meta strings joined with middle dots, labels built as a word plus a spaced em dash plus a fragment, and an arrow appended to every link and button.
- Title Case on every heading and button of a web product that has not chosen it.
- Vague aspirational headlines, superlatives, hedging, and generic calls to action such as "Get started" or "Submit" where a specific verb fits.

## Functional defaults

- Only the populated happy path is designed. Empty, loading, partial, error, offline, and permission states are missing or generic.
- Focus indicators removed or invisible, hover as the only cue, and targets smaller than the platform minimum.
- Light gray secondary text that fails contrast, often on a tinted surface.
- A dark theme that was never inspected, or one produced by inverting the light theme.
- A spinner in the middle of content where a layout-preserving placeholder belongs, and content that jumps when data arrives.
- Everything visible at once instead of progressive disclosure.
- Equal visual weight on every element, so nothing leads.

## Replace a default

Return to the subject's world and its real content, and change typography first, because it carries the most personality per decision and tends to lift other choices with it. Spend boldness in one place and keep the rest disciplined, because several loud elements cancel each other.

<example>
A seed-catalog site sets its headings in a sturdy slab taken from old packet labels and keeps the rest to one green and plain white space.
</example>

On Operate surfaces, replace a default with precision and not novelty, such as a deliberate type scale, aligned edges, tabular numerals, complete states, and a restrained accent with one job, because users repeat the task daily and reward predictability.

<example>
An inventory table replaces its gradient header with a `1.125` type scale, right-aligned tabular numerals, and a single blue accent used only for the selected row.
</example>
